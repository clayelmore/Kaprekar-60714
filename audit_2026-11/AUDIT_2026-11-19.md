## 2. Claims that are now stronger than the paper states

* **Dyadic tower level 3 (Conjecture 6.6, j=3) is settled by complete enumeration.** All 350,343,555
  non-repdigit 32-digit multisets reach 1746 written eight times. The paper lists this as Evidence
  (200,000 samples). The direct 8-fold fails (99.9826 %), as the paper says.
* **m=7 clean repeat is settled by complete enumeration.** Under the rule in `m7_witness.json`, all
  124,403,610 non-repdigit 28-digit multisets reach 6174 written seven times (`m7_full.txt`). The paper
  lists this as Evidence (500,000 samples).
* The 1746 6-fold (d=24) and 7-fold (d=28) failures are now complete enumerations (83.76 %, 55.12 %).

## 3. Problems found

1. **The 60714 escape class is not thin.** The theorem as stated is true (every orbit ends at 60714 or 0,
   no cycles, d=5..24), but the share ending at 0 grows with d:

   | d | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 | 24 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | multisets collapsing to 0 | 0.56 % | 3.12 % | 6.66 % | 9.72 % | 12.06 % |
   | share of all d-digit strings collapsing to 0 | 0.03 % | 3.05 % | 9.88 % | 17.8 % | 24.3 % |

   The introduction's "a thin set" and the README's "universal attractor at every digit length" overstate this.
2. **Unreproduced number.** "481 under the strict near-repdigit convention" (Computed 5.6): the full search
   gives 465 under both conventions.
3. **Induction wording (minor).** Lemma 4.3 and E.4 are phrased for admissible states, but orbits pass through
   near-repdigits at length d as well. Stating the induction for all non-repdigit multisets (true at the roots:
   all 1,992 and 4,995 converge) removes the need for the projection lemma. No error in the result.
4. **Citations.** Kaprekar 1955 is Scripta Math. 21, p. 304 (15:244-245 is the 1949 "Another solitaire game").
   The 5-digit precursor is Prichett, J. Reine Angew. Math. 303/304 (1978) 379-388 (the cited title is Trigg's,
   Math. Magazine 45 (1972)). Peterson & Pulapaka is K. Peterson and H. Pulapaka, "The Kaprekar Routine and
   Other Digit Games for Undergraduate Exploration," J. Math. Sci.: Collaborative Explorations 10 (2008)
   143-156. Devlin & Zeng is Integers 21 #A97, first author P. Devlin.
5. **Prior work on the family.** Peterson & Pulapaka (2008) already define the "Kaprekar routine of type
   (alpha, beta)" for two permutations, count m!(m!-1)/2 maps (the paper's d!(d!-1) counts each map twice),
   and pose the 4-digit classification as an open problem. Also relevant: Chaille (Stetson thesis 2006, unread),
   Bajorska-Harapinska et al. (2017), Smarandache (arXiv:1005.3235). No source found names 1746, 2538, 5382,
   60714 or 146070 as attractors; OEIS has nothing.

Not rechecked: the reaching-time table E.3, the level-by-level content of Lemma 6.9 / Prop 6.10 / Computed
6.12 beyond running `verify_tower.py`, and the [S]-marked counts at m >= 3.

## 4. New findings

### 4.1 146070 is universal with no escape class at every length tested (d = 6..24)

Rule at length d (coefficients on the sorted digits s_0 >= ... >= s_{d-1}):

    c = (990, 90000, -99990, -900, -99, 999999, 9*10^6, 9*10^7, ..., 9*10^(d-2), -(10^(d-1) - 10^4))

(place form: a = (3,5,1,2,0,6,7,...,d-1,4), b = (1,4,5,3,2,0,6,...,d-1); at d=6 the tail is just 9999.)
Every non-repdigit multiset, near-repdigits included, reaches 146070 at each d from 6 to 24 (38,567,090
multisets at d=24), and the maximum number of steps to the target digits is 41 for 8 <= d <= 50 (42 to the integer 146070 itself; see 4.7), then grows like d/5 + 31
(`ladder146070.py`, `big146070.py`). A computer-assisted proof for every d >= 6 is in `PROOF_146070.md`.
Why it is promising for a proof: the digit the rule writes at place k >= 6 is the gap s_{k-1} - s_k, and the
gaps sum to at most 9, so after one step almost all high digits are zero whatever d is.
Note 60714 itself cannot do this: no full-variable rule makes it universal at d=7 or d=8.

### 4.2 The mixed chain holds at every length from 4 to 18

Target: digits {1,4,6,7} each repeated floor(d/4) times, plus d mod 4 zeros. A universal fixed point with
exactly that digit content exists at every d = 4..18 (all non-repdigit multisets, independent engine confirms
one rule per length):

| d | digit content | universal rules found (up to sign) | distinct fixed integers | search |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 7641 | 2 | 2 | complete |
| 5 | 76410 | 2 | 2 | complete |

| 7 | 7641000 | 6 | 5 | complete |

| 9 | 776644110 | 10,151 | 5,045 | complete |

The specific ordering 66771144 is not universal under any rule; 77664411 is, and at d=10 so is 0011446677.

### 4.4 Control experiment: how special is "a universal fixed point exists with this digit content"?

Share of eligible digit sets (digit sum divisible by 9, not (near-)repdigit) that carry at least one universal
full-variable fixed point: d=4: 2 of 67 (3.0 %); d=5: 8 of 212 (3.8 %); d=6: 55 of 547 (10.1 %);
d=7: 348 of 1,260 (27.6 %, repo catalog); d=8: 12 of 30 randomly chosen (`control.py 8 30 11`), and those 12
are exactly the sets that have any fixed arrangement at all. So existence at a single length becomes generic
as d grows; the mixed chain of 4.2 at d >= 8 is expected rather than special. What is not generic is a single
recipe working at every length (4.1, the folds, the dyadic tower).

### 4.5 Unbiased census of all six-letter cores (no digit set assumed)

Extension recipe: a = (a_0..a_4, 6, 7, ..., d-1, a_5), b = (b_0..b_4, b_5, 6, ..., d-1), i.e. the extra sorted digits
are written in reverse order in front of both rearrangements, offset by one place. `closure_all.c` runs the
general form of the argument in `PROOF_146070.md` on all 190,800 full-variable ordered cores (a,b):
551 pass (15,120 fail Lemma 0), on 57 nonzero-digit sets (`closure_all_out.txt`, `all_cores_pass.txt`).
Complete enumeration of lengths 6..20 for all 551, and 21 for the 14 whose closure reaches 21 nonzero digits
(`cores_short_lengths.py`, `all_cores_summary.txt`): every one is universal for a single fixed integer at every
length from d0 on, with d0 = 6 (75 cores), 7 (105), 8 (256), 9 (97), 10 (18). The 75 with d0 = 6 are exactly
the survivors found independently by `ladder_rarity.py`.

Families by number of nonzero digits: 3 digits: only {9,5,4} (digits of 495; 2 cores, endpoint 9045, d0 = 7);
4 digits: only {7,6,4,1} (1 core, 146070, d0 = 6) and {9,9,8,1} (100 cores; digits of 10989); the remaining
54 families have 5 to 8 nonzero digits (largest: {7,4,3,2,1,1} 89 cores, {8,4,3,2,1} 36, {9,5,5,4,4} 32).
Scope: one recipe, six-letter cores only. The all-lengths argument was checked by an adversarial AI referee
(`referee/`, verdict sound with fixable gaps; gaps fixed in `PROOF_146070.md`, including the restated drain for
cores with a_5 = 0) and by a blind reimplementation (`replication/`, 30 sampled cores, all numbers agree).
No human mathematician has reviewed it.

## 4.6 Five-letter census (2026-11-21)

Question: can a five-letter rule, extended by the same recipe (extras in front, reversed, offset by one), keep
6174's digits with a single core zero (the 60714 form) at every length?  **No.**

- All 5,280 full-variable five-letter cores were tested two ways: `closure_all5.c` (the all-lengths check adapted
  from `closure_all.c`: 4 free coefficients, low part mod 10^5) and `census5_brute.py` (complete enumeration of
  every length 5..12 for every core).  Both give the same **17 cores**; those 17 were then enumerated at every
  length up to 20 (`cores5_pass_to20.txt`): 7 are universal from length 7, 10 from length 8.
- Digit sets of the 17: 6 5 4 2 1 (5 rules), 7 5 3 2 1 (4), 8 7 6 3 2 1 (3), 8 7 6 5 1 (2), 8 4 3 2 1 (2),
  9 7 6 3 2 (1).  None has four or fewer nonzero digits; 8 4 3 2 1 also appears in the six-letter census.
- Digits 1,4,6,7: four cores are universal at length 5 (60714 twice, 60417 twice); one of them
  (ADCBE - DEACB) is also universal at length 6 with endpoint 60714.  From length 7 on all four are captured by
  cycles (60714 or 60417 stays a fixed point with a small basin, about 4% at length 12).
- Status: the all-lengths argument for five-letter cores is the six-letter proof with constants changed; it has
  not been written out or refereed.  The enumeration to length 20 is complete.  Other extension recipes
  (e.g. the paper's alternating ladder) are outside this census.

## 4.7 Third review of the 146070 proof (2026-11-21)

A separate AI session reviewed PROOF_146070.md, reran the programs and reimplemented the closure by direct
subtraction (2,941,883 seeds, 2,942,591 states, max 20 nonzero digits: all agree).  Verdict: no counterexample,
no fatal flaw, three repairs, all now applied to the proof:
1. Step counts were distances to the target digit multiset, not to the integer.  67140 has the target digits;
   one more step gives 146070.  Reproduced here: 10000000 at d=8 takes 42 steps.  Every "41 steps" figure in this
   audit, the drawings and `kap` output is a multiset distance; add 1 for the integer in the worst case.
2. The assembly now excludes repdigit images explicitly (new Lemma 0').
3. Exact bound: floor((d-1)/5) + 43 steps (safe, not sharp).
It also supplied a computation-free proof of Lemma 0 (added).  The review did not cover novelty or the census.

`censusN.c` (compile with -DN=3..7) runs two independent tests on every full-variable N-letter core: A, complete
enumeration at length N+6; B, the all-lengths check (seeds: at most N-1 nines and 9+N other nonzero digits;
covers d >= max(2N+9, M+1)).  A and B agree on every core at N = 5, 6 and 7.

## 4.9 Structure search: clusters, anagram additions, carries, duality (2026-11-21)

- Clusters (`clusters.py`): neighbors = swap the places of two adjacent letters in one word.  N=6: 508 clusters,
  largest 3.  N=7: 20,910 clusters, largest 8; 88% of survivors isolated.  Half of the edges keep the endpoint
  unchanged (repeated digits: a zero gap makes that gap weight irrelevant to the fixed point).  No large families.
- Feature scan (`structure_scan.py`, all 190,800 six-letter cores): the only absolute condition found is that no
  partial sum of coefficients is zero (0 of 15,120 survive).  Kendall distance between the words, sign patterns,
  weight sizes, closing places: survival 2-5 per 1,000 throughout.
- Anagram addition: every endpoint F satisfies F + W_lo = W_hi with all three numbers arrangements of the same
  digits (146070 + 461070 = 607140).  Hence digit sum of F = 9 x (number of carries in that addition), by the
  base-10 form of Kummer's carry theorem.  Verified on all 22,994 catalog rows: carries 1 (9 rules), 2 (3,492),
  3 (12,465), 4 (6,771), 5 (253), 6 (4).  Elementary; not claimed new.
- Self-coordinates: F = sum_j g_j C_j, g_j the gaps of F's own sorted digits, C_j the rule's partial sums.
- Duality (`duality.py`): W_hi - W_lo = F iff W_hi - F = W_lo, so each fixed point has a dual rule with fixed
  point W_lo.  Tested at d=6 on the 1,174 universal rules: 20 of 2,686 duals universal (0.7%, the base rate).
  Duality pairs fixed points but does NOT carry universality.
- Gap shapes of endpoints (N=7 rows): the common shapes are staircases of 1s and 2s; not yet compared to a baseline.
