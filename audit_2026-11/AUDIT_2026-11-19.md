## 2. Claims that are now stronger than the paper states

* **Dyadic tower level 3 (Conjecture 6.6, j=3) is settled by complete enumeration.** All 350,343,555
  non-repdigit 32-digit multisets reach 1746 written eight times. The paper lists this as Evidence
  (200,000 samples). The direct 8-fold fails (99.9826 %), as the paper says.
* **m=7 clean repeat is settled by complete enumeration.** Under the rule in `m7_witness.json`, all
  124,403,610 non-repdigit 28-digit multisets reach 6174 written seven times (`m7_full.txt`). The paper
  lists this as Evidence (500,000 samples).
* The 1746 6-fold (d=24) and 7-fold (d=28) failures are now complete enumerations (83.76 %, 55.12 %).

## 3. Problems found

1. **The 60714 escape class is not thin.** The theorem as stated is true (every orbit ends at 60714 or 0,
   no cycles, d=5..24), but the share ending at 0 grows with d:

   | d | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 | 24 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | multisets collapsing to 0 | 0.56 % | 3.12 % | 6.66 % | 9.72 % | 12.06 % |
   | share of all d-digit strings collapsing to 0 | 0.03 % | 3.05 % | 9.88 % | 17.8 % | 24.3 % |

   The introduction's "a thin set" and the README's "universal attractor at every digit length" overstate this.
2. **Unreproduced number.** "481 under the strict near-repdigit convention" (Computed 5.6): the full search
   gives 465 under both conventions.
3. **Induction wording (minor).** Lemma 4.3 and E.4 are phrased for admissible states, but orbits pass through
   near-repdigits at length d as well. Stating the induction for all non-repdigit multisets (true at the roots:
   all 1,992 and 4,995 converge) removes the need for the projection lemma. No error in the result.
4. **Citations.** Kaprekar 1955 is Scripta Math. 21, p. 304 (15:244-245 is the 1949 "Another solitaire game").
   The 5-digit precursor is Prichett, J. Reine Angew. Math. 303/304 (1978) 379-388 (the cited title is Trigg's,
   Math. Magazine 45 (1972)). Peterson & Pulapaka is K. Peterson and H. Pulapaka, "The Kaprekar Routine and
   Other Digit Games for Undergraduate Exploration," J. Math. Sci.: Collaborative Explorations 10 (2008)
   143-156. Devlin & Zeng is Integers 21 #A97, first author P. Devlin.
5. **Prior work on the family.** Peterson & Pulapaka (2008) already define the "Kaprekar routine of type
   (alpha, beta)" for two permutations, count m!(m!-1)/2 maps (the paper's d!(d!-1) counts each map twice),
   and pose the 4-digit classification as an open problem. Also relevant: Chaille (Stetson thesis 2006, unread),
   Bajorska-Harapinska et al. (2017), Smarandache (arXiv:1005.3235). No source found names 1746, 2538, 5382,
   60714 or 146070 as attractors; OEIS has nothing.

Not rechecked: the reaching-time table E.3, the level-by-level content of Lemma 6.9 / Prop 6.10 / Computed
6.12 beyond running `verify_tower.py`, and the [S]-marked counts at m >= 3.

## 4. New findings

### 4.1 146070 is universal with no escape class at every length tested (d = 6..24)

Rule at length d (coefficients on the sorted digits s_0 >= ... >= s_{d-1}):

    c = (990, 90000, -99990, -900, -99, 999999, 9*10^6, 9*10^7, ..., 9*10^(d-2), -(10^(d-1) - 10^4))

(place form: a = (3,5,1,2,0,6,7,...,d-1,4), b = (1,4,5,3,2,0,6,...,d-1); at d=6 the tail is just 9999.)
Every non-repdigit multiset, near-repdigits included, reaches 146070 at each d from 6 to 24 (38,567,090
multisets at d=24), and the maximum number of steps to the target digits is 41 for 8 <= d <= 50 (42 to the integer 146070 itself; see 4.7), then grows like d/5 + 31
(`ladder146070.py`, `big146070.py`). A computer-assisted proof for every d >= 6 is in `PROOF_146070.md`.
Why it is promising for a proof: the digit the rule writes at place k >= 6 is the gap s_{k-1} - s_k, and the
gaps sum to at most 9, so after one step almost all high digits are zero whatever d is.
Note 60714 itself cannot do this: no full-variable rule makes it universal at d=7 or d=8.

### 4.4 Control experiment: how special is "a universal fixed point exists with this digit content"?

Share of eligible digit sets (digit sum divisible by 9, not (near-)repdigit) that carry at least one universal
full-variable fixed point: d=4: 2 of 67 (3.0 %); d=5: 8 of 212 (3.8 %); d=6: 55 of 547 (10.1 %);
d=7: 348 of 1,260 (27.6 %, repo catalog); d=8: 12 of 30 randomly chosen (`control.py 8 30 11`), and those 12
are exactly the sets that have any fixed arrangement at all. So existence at a single length becomes generic
as d grows; the mixed chain of 4.2 at d >= 8 is expected rather than special. What is not generic is a single
recipe working at every length (4.1, the folds, the dyadic tower).

## 4.9 Structure search: clusters, anagram additions, carries, duality (2026-11-21)

- Clusters (`clusters.py`): neighbors = swap the places of two adjacent letters in one word.  N=6: 508 clusters,
  largest 3.  N=7: 20,910 clusters, largest 8; 88% of survivors isolated.  Half of the edges keep the endpoint
  unchanged (repeated digits: a zero gap makes that gap weight irrelevant to the fixed point).  No large families.
- Feature scan (`structure_scan.py`, all 190,800 six-letter cores): the only absolute condition found is that no
  partial sum of coefficients is zero (0 of 15,120 survive).  Kendall distance between the words, sign patterns,
  weight sizes, closing places: survival 2-5 per 1,000 throughout.
- Anagram addition: every endpoint F satisfies F + W_lo = W_hi with all three numbers arrangements of the same
  digits (146070 + 461070 = 607140).  Hence digit sum of F = 9 x (number of carries in that addition), by the
  base-10 form of Kummer's carry theorem.  Verified on all 22,994 catalog rows: carries 1 (9 rules), 2 (3,492),
  3 (12,465), 4 (6,771), 5 (253), 6 (4).  Elementary; not claimed new.
- Self-coordinates: F = sum_j g_j C_j, g_j the gaps of F's own sorted digits, C_j the rule's partial sums.
- Duality (`duality.py`): W_hi - W_lo = F iff W_hi - F = W_lo, so each fixed point has a dual rule with fixed
  point W_lo.  Tested at d=6 on the 1,174 universal rules: 20 of 2,686 duals universal (0.7%, the base rate).
  Duality pairs fixed points but does NOT carry universality.
- Gap shapes of endpoints (N=7 rows): the common shapes are staircases of 1s and 2s; not yet compared to a baseline.

## 4.11 Proof: a six-digit constant in every base (2026-11-21)

`PROOF_ALLBASE_D6.md`: for every base b >= 3 the six-digit rule EABDFC - DFCEAB sends every non-repdigit to
(b-1)(b^3-1) (base 10: 8991) in at most b/2 + 10 steps.  Method: K = (b^3-1) m, so an image is three digits plus
their complements; the dynamics becomes a map on two integers (A, w); every case reaches the target in <= 3 steps
except chains of one case, which satisfy omega_{k+1} + omega_k = -2 - 2|omega_{k-1}| and so cannot last.
Machine-checked: reduction vs direct rule on every six-digit string for bases 3..14; reduced map for every
state, bases 3..200; the chain identity for bases 3..399.  Independent AI referee (`referee_allbase/`): sound with fixable gaps, all fixed; its own code confirms bases 3..80 by the direct rule.
NOT proved: the same rule at lengths >= 7 in every base (conjecture; tested as in 4.10).

## 4.12 Half-swap rules and a constant at every even length >= 6 in every base (2026-11-21)

`halfswap.c`: a half-swap rule at length 2n moves every letter exactly n places, so K = (b^n - 1) m.
Census of all (2n)! half-swap rules by complete enumeration at length 2n:
- n=1: none universal in any base 3-16.  n=2: irregular (4 or 12 rules in bases 3, 5, 6, 11, 12; none in 4, 7-10, 16).
- n=3: exactly 24 rules, the same 24 in every base 7-16, all on the pairing {AF, BC, DE}.
- n=4: 224 rules universal in all of bases 3-12 and 16 (pairings {AG,BH,CD,EF} and {AH,BG,CD,EF}).
- n=5: 238,860 (base 7) and 104,618+ (base 10); 67,680 in both.  No universal rule pairs A with B.
`PROOF_HALFSWAP.md`: an explicit family R_n (one rule per n >= 3) with endpoint (b-1)(b^n-1) in every base
b >= 3: base 10 gives 8991, 89991, 899991, ...  Proof by reduction to a map on "deficits" of n folded digits.
Even n needs at most 7 steps; odd n >= 5 at most b/3 + 9; n = 3 uses PROOF_ALLBASE_D6.
Machine checks: reduction exact on every string for n = 3..8 in small bases; reduced maps for n up to 15 and
bases up to 1001; direct enumeration of R_n for n = 3..9 in bases 3-13.  Independent AI referee (`referee_halfswap/`): sound with fixable gaps, all fixed (the real one: no first image is a repdigit, now section 1.5); its own code confirms n = 3..8.
Not covered: odd lengths, lengths 2 and 4, and a single rule that works at every length (R_n depends on n).

## 4.13 Odd lengths: a working family, not yet proved (2026-11-21)

`oddswap.c`: at length 2n+1, places q and q+n are swapped for q = 1..n-1 and places 2n, n, 0 carry a 3-cycle, so
every coefficient is a multiple of b^n - 1 and no letter is ignored.  Census of all (2n+1)! such rules:
- length 5: none universal in every base (1 rule in each of bases 10, 11, 12, 16; more in small bases).
- length 7: 22 rules universal in every base 3-12 and 16.
- length 9: 1,401 rules universal in all of bases 4, 5, 7, 10, 13; 40 of them end at (b-1)(b^4-1).
Family U_n (`oddlen_family.py`, specs in `oddswap/familyU.txt`): take the even-length rule with place-0 pair
(n, n+1), replace letter 2n-1 by 2n in its pair, and turn the place-0 pair into the 3-cycle (2n-1, n, n+1).
Complete enumeration: U_n is universal with endpoint (b-1)(b^n-1) for n = 3, 4, 5, 6 in bases 3, 4, 5, 7, 10, 13
and n = 7 in bases 3, 4, 5, 7, 10 (lengths 7, 9, 11, 13, 15).
NOT PROVED.  What carries over: K = (b^n-1) m, and m = 0 only for repdigits.  What does not: m can be as large
as b^(n+1), and then an image is no longer n digits plus their complements; one complementary pair is replaced
by a triple {t, y0 - t, b-1-y0} (or a borrow variant).  Orbits move in and out of the mirror-symmetric shape
(e.g. base 10, n = 3: m = 4118, 1329, 1039, 8, 108, 1009, 1110, 910, 90, 9), so the deficit map of
PROOF_HALFSWAP.md does not apply.  Of the 22 length-7 rules, GAF,E-D,B-C leaves the non-symmetric shape
soonest (by the third image in bases 6, 9, 10); it is the best candidate for a proof but has a different endpoint.

## 4.14 Odd lengths: passenger-digit rule, endgame proved, descent open (2026-11-21)

Details in `NOTES_ODD_LENGTHS.md`; checks in `oddpass.py`, `oddscan.py`, `oddswap/familyG_check.txt`.
- True depths (not the `maxsteps` path length printed by the C census) grow with the base for every one of the
  22 length-7 rules and the best length-9 rules; U_n of 4.13 grows linearly. No bounded-step proof like the
  even-length one is possible for these rules.
- Rule G_n (n odd): smallest letter is the extra digit; G_n = E_n on the top 2n digits minus b^n x (bottom gap),
  where E_n is a universal even half-swap rule built from neighboring pairs. n = 3 is GAF,E-D,C-B.
- PROVED: factorization; zero only on repdigits; every image is one triple summing to b-1 plus n-1
  complementary pairs (single shape, exact formula); if the two smallest digits are equal the step equals the
  even rule's; any string with two 0s and a b-1 reaches F = (b^n-1)(b^{k+1}+b^k-b+1), k = (n-1)/2, in <= 4 steps.
  Base 10: 100899, 109098909, 109909989009 at lengths 7, 11, 15.
- NOT PROVED: every non-repdigit reaches a string with two 0s and a b-1. Enumeration: universal for n = 3, 5, 7
  in bases 3, 4, 5, 7, 10, 13. The descent is Euclid-like (depth ~ log b); seven candidate monovariants fail
  over 1 and 2 steps; the sum of deficits did not increase over 3 steps in n = 5, 7 samples (lead, unproved).
- Even n (lengths 9, 13, ...) has no tested general family in passenger form yet.
- Follow-up the same day: the 3-step sum-of-deficits lead is false (counterexample n = 5, base 97). The gap-based
  total T = b-1 - (even-numbered gaps) held over 3 steps in n = 5, 7 samples but fails at n = 3. Descent still open.
- Odd-length INPUTS are already covered by the even theorem via one leading zero (5 digits -> 8991, 7 -> 89991,
  9 -> 899991 in base 10; enumerated). This is a different process from an odd-length rule; see NOTES section 5b.

## 4.15 Fixed-middle rule: odd lengths proved, one scheme for every length >= 6 (2026-11-21)

`PROOF_FIXEDMIDDLE.md`, `midfix.c`, `midfix_family.py`, `midfix_checks.py`, outputs in `midfix/`.
- Parity fact: an odd number of places cannot be split into exchanged pairs, so an odd-length rule must keep one
  letter in place (as Kaprekar's 495 does) or rotate three or more letters (4.13-4.14, triple images, unproved).
- Rule M_n (length 2n+1): letter s_1 stays at the middle place n; the other 2n letters carry R_n with swap
  distance n+1.  K = (b^(n+1) - 1) m with m < b^n, so every image is one digit b-1 plus n complementary pairs.
- Unified statement: length L >= 6, h = ceil(L/2), exchange place q with q+h; endpoint (b-1)(b^h - 1).
  Base 10: lengths 7, 8 -> 89991; 9, 10 -> 899991; 11, 12 -> 8999991.
- Proof: reduced deficit map as in the even case with one term changed; bad runs bounded by H (n odd, n >= 6
  even) or b-3 (n = 4).  Step bounds b/2 + 3, b/2 + 5, b + 2.
- Enumeration: single endpoint for n = 3..6 in bases 3, 4, 5, 7, 10, 13 and n = 7 in bases 3, 4, 5, 7.
- Census at length 7: 48 fixed-middle rules work in all of bases 3-12 and 16 (fixed letter always s_1 or s_2);
  at length 5 none works in every base.
- Refereed (independent AI pass, `referee_fixedmiddle/`): sound, with four presentation gaps, all fixed (step count
  for b = 3, 4; borrow shown explicitly in 3.1; w_j <= H stated; sharper run bound). No human review.
- Length 9 census (bases 4, 7, 10): 5,224 fixed-middle rules work in all three; fixed letter never s_0 or s_2n.

## 4.17 Usage scores: how much of the number a rule uses (2026-11-21)

`usage_score.py`, outputs `usage_scores_b10.tsv`, `usage_scores_b7.tsv`.  For K = |sum C_j g_j| over the L-1 gaps:
letters = letters with nonzero coefficient; gaps = number of distinct nonzero |C_j| (separate quantities step one

between the extreme-swap rule and 6174; the base-10 rules 60714 and 146070 sit above 6174 on every measure
except endpoint richness.  The measures are descriptive, chosen by me, and not a theorem.

## 4.22 Build-to-order survey: which target numbers can be endpoints? (2026-11-21)

## 4.23 Recipe hunt: what distinguishes rules that work? (2026-11-21)

`recipe_hunt.py` -> `to_order/hunt_L10.tsv`.  120 ten-digit targets, 20,000 sampled fixing rules each; 1,902
winners (complete check) against about 403,000 losers (weighted sample); base rate 0.47 %.
- One hard law, with a one-line proof: if a gap weight C_j = c_0 + ... + c_j is 0, the string with j+1 ones
  followed by zeros maps to 0, so the rule has no universal endpoint.  In the data: 0 winners among 68,650
  rules with a zero gap weight (this includes "largest letter stays put", C_0 = 0).
- Nothing else separates winners from losers.  Number of negative gap weights, sizes of the weights, where the
  largest and smallest letters sit, number of fixed letters, how sorted the two words are, number of carries:
  every value has a success rate within about a factor of 2 of the base rate.
- A constant-chance model (each fixing rule succeeds independently with one probability per length) fits badly:
  targets with few rules succeed much more often than it predicts.  The chance per rule depends on the target.
- Conclusion: no recipe found.  Winners look like ordinary rules that happen to have no second fixed point and
  no cycle.  The conjecture of 4.22 has no constructive handle yet.
