# 146070 is a universal fixed point at every length d >= 6 (computer-assisted proof, 2026-11-19)

Status: derived and machine-checked on 2026-11-19, then checked the same day by two independent AI passes (an
adversarial referee, files in `referee/`, verdict "sound with fixable gaps", all gaps fixed below; and a blind
reimplementation, files in `replication/`, all numbers agree). A third independent AI review on 2026-11-21
(separate session, own closure reimplementation by direct subtraction) found no counterexample and asked for
three repairs, all applied below: the final step from the target digits to the integer, the exclusion of
repdigit images in the assembly, and an exact step bound. Not yet reviewed by a human mathematician.

## Statement

For d >= 6 let s_0 >= s_1 >= ... >= s_{d-1} be the digits of a d-digit string (leading zeros allowed) sorted
descending, and define

    K_d(s) = | 990 s_0 + 90000 s_1 - 99990 s_2 - 900 s_3 - 99 s_4 + 999999 s_5
               + 9*10^6 s_6 + 9*10^7 s_7 + ... + 9*10^(d-2) s_(d-2) - (10^(d-1) - 10^4) s_(d-1) |

(at d = 6 the last two listed terms merge into 9999 s_5). This is a generalized Kaprekar rule: the difference
of two rearrangements of the sorted digits, place maps a = (3,5,1,2,0,6,...,d-1,4), b = (1,4,5,3,2,0,6,...,d-1).

**Theorem.** For every d >= 6, every d-digit string that is not a repdigit reaches 146070 under iteration of
K_d, in at most floor((d-1)/5) + 43 steps (a safe bound, not claimed sharp). Repdigits map to 0.

## Proof

**Step 0 (gap form).** With gaps g_j = s_j - s_{j+1} >= 0,

    K_d(s) = | H + L |,   H = sum_{k=6}^{d-1} 10^k g_{k-1},
    L = 990 s_0 + 90000 s_1 - 99990 s_2 - 900 s_3 - 99 s_4 - s_5 + 10^4 s_{d-1},   -90000 <= L <= 818910.

So the digit written at place k >= 6 is the gap g_{k-1}. The gaps are single digits with sum <= 9.
H is either 0 or at least 10^6, so H + L > 0 whenever H > 0 and the absolute value matters only when H = 0.
Three cases: (i) H = 0: the result is |L|, at most six digits, no borrow. (ii) H > 0, L >= 0: the low six
places hold L. (iii) H > 0, L < 0: a borrow replaces the lowest nonzero gap digit g by g-1, fills every
place between it and place 6 with 9, and the low six places hold 10^6 - |L|.

**Lemma 0.** K_d(s) = 0 only for repdigits. (If H > 0 then K_d(s) >= 10^6 - 90000 > 0. H = 0 forces s_5 = ... = s_{d-1}; then L = 0 is a condition on
six sorted digits, checked exhaustively: no non-constant solution. `lemma_checks.py`.)
Proof without computation (third review): with H = 0, L/9 = 110 g_0 + 10110 g_1 - 1000 g_2 - 1100 g_3 - 1111 g_4.
Modulo 10 this is -g_4, so g_4 = 0. Dividing by 10, 11 g_0 + 1011 g_1 - 100 g_2 - 110 g_3 = 0; modulo 10,
g_0 + g_1 = 0 (the gap sum is at most 9). Then 100 g_2 + 110 g_3 = 0 forces g_2 = g_3 = 0: all digits equal.

**Lemma 0' (no repdigit images, d >= 21).** The image of a non-repdigit is never a repdigit. It is not 0 by
Lemma 0. It is not 9^d: the value 10^d - 1 is not a difference of two rearrangements of the same digits (that
would need one of them to be all 9s and the other all 0s). It is not r^d for r in 1..8: that would be more
than 15 nonzero digits other than 9, contradicting Lemma 1.

**Lemma 1 (one step lands in a thin family).** For any s, the nonzero digits of K_d(s) are: possibly a run
of 9s from the borrow, at most 9 gap digits, and at most 6 low digits. Collect ALL the 9s (from the run, and
any gap or low digit that happens to be 9) as 9^t and call the other nonzero digits R. Then
K_d(s) = 9^t + R + zeros with |R| <= 15. Immediate from Step 0. In fact |R| <= 11 for every d: the multiset R
depends only on (s_0, ..., s_5, s_{d-1}) and the nonzero gaps in order, every such configuration already occurs
at d = 16, and complete enumeration at d = 16 (and at every d from 12 to 30, fourth review) gives max |R| = 11.

**Lemma 2 (nine-drain).** Let the state be 9^t + R + zeros with t >= 6 and R made of digits 1..8.
Then s_0 = ... = s_5 = 9, so L = -90000 + 10^4 m <= -10000 < 0 (m = s_{d-1} <= 8); and since the state is not
9^d, the gap g_{t-1} = 9 - s_t >= 1 sits at place t >= 6, so H > 0. This is the lowest nonzero gap, and the
image is

    9^(t-5) + R' + zeros,   R' a multiset of digits 1..8 summing to 9,

with at least four zeros, in each of these cases (m = smallest digit of the state):
  (a) the state has a zero (m = 0): low places read 9,1,0,0,0,0 and R' = {8 - r_max, gaps of R, r_min, 1}
      (for R empty, R' = {8, 1});
  (b) zero-free, m in 1..7: low places read 9, 1+m, 0,0,0,0 and R' = {8 - r_max, gaps of R, 1+m};
  (c) zero-free, m = 8 (so R is all 8s): the image is 9^(t-4) + zeros, with R' empty.
So each step with t >= 6 lowers the count of 9s by exactly 5, except case (c), which lowers it by 4 and can
only be the first step (every image has at least four zeros). The count never increases and the drain ends
at some t in 1..5 (from t >= 6: t-5 >= 1, t-4 >= 2), with at most 9 other nonzero digits.
Proof: Step 0 case (iii) with L = -90000 + 10^4 m. The borrow turns g_{t-1} into g_{t-1} - 1 <= 8, writes t - 6
nines at places 6..t-1, and the low six places hold 10^6 - |L| = 910000 + 10^4 m, read 9, 1+m, 0, 0, 0, 0. The
other gaps are unchanged; listing the nonzero ones gives (a), (b), (c), and their sum is 9 in (a) and (b).
Cross-check: exact R' comparison on 71,536 cases here, 465,396 by the referee, and about 540,000 at d = 21 to
400 in the fourth review, with no failure.

**Lemma 3 (length-free dynamics).** If a state has at least one zero digit, the closing coefficient
multiplies 0 and K_d depends only on the nonzero digits: K_d(s) = |V(N)| with
V(N) = 990 n_0 + 90000 n_1 - 99990 n_2 - 900 n_3 - 99 n_4 - n_5 + sum_{j>=6} 10^j (n_{j-1} - n_j),
N the nonzero digits padded with zeros. Call the induced map on nonzero-digit multisets Phi; it does not
involve d at all.

**Finite check.** Let S be the set of all nonempty multisets of nonzero digits with at most five 9s and at
most fifteen other digits (2,941,883 multisets). Following Phi from every member of S: all of them reach
{7,6,4,1} (the nonzero digits of 146070), none reaches 0 or a cycle, the longest orbit has 41 steps to that
digit multiset, and no orbit ever visits a multiset with more than 20 digits. (`closure.c`, run as
`./closure 5 15`.) Note the distance is to the multiset, not to the integer: 67140 already has the target
digits, and one more step gives 146070.

**Assembly, d >= 21.** Take any non-repdigit s. By Lemmas 0-1 its image is 9^t + R + zeros with |R| <= 15,
and by Lemma 0' that image is not a repdigit (in particular not 9^d, which Lemma 2 excludes).
If t >= 6, Lemma 2 applies repeatedly (zeros are present after the first drain step) until 1 <= t <= 5 with
|R| <= 9. Either way the orbit arrives at a state whose nonzero digits form a member of S; it has at most
20 nonzero digits, so at least one zero since d >= 21. By the finite check its Phi-orbit stays within 20
nonzero digits, so every later state also has a zero, Lemma 3 keeps applying, and the orbit reaches
{7,6,4,1} + zeros. A state with those digits is some arrangement of them (for example 67140), and one more
step maps it to K_d({7,6,4,1} + zeros) = 990*7 + 90000*6 - 99990*4 - 900*1 = 146070, which is fixed.
Steps: 1 initial + at most floor((d-1)/5) drain steps (t <= d-1, each drain step needs t >= 6 and removes 5
nines, or 4 once) + at most 41 to the target digits + 1 to the integer = floor((d-1)/5) + 43.
In detail: from t nines, k drain steps leave at most t - 5k + 1 nines, and the drain stops once this is <= 5.
So k <= ceil((t-4)/5) = floor(t/5) <= floor((d-1)/5). For 6 <= d <= 20 the bound is checked by enumeration
(maximum 42 <= 44), not derived.

**d = 6..24.** Complete enumeration of every non-repdigit multiset at each length (`ladder146070.py`;
38,567,090 multisets at d = 24): all reach 146070, in at most 41 steps to the target digits (42 to the
integer; for example 10000000 at d = 8 takes 42), within the bound. The ranges overlap at d = 21..24.  QED

Empirical cross-check at long lengths (`big146070.py`): no failures at d = 30, 50, 100, 200, 400, 1000;
observed maximum steps to the target digits 41, 41, 51, 71, 111, 231, i.e. d/5 + 31 once d >= 100 (add 1 for
the integer).

## Context

* Prichett, Ludington and Lapenta (1981) proved the classical routine has a Kaprekar constant only at 3 digits
  (495) and 4 digits (6174). The theorem above shows that one explicit rule from the two-rearrangement family
  has a constant at every length >= 6, built from the digits of 6174 plus zeros.
* The same extension recipe keeps 75 of the 720 eligible universal 6-digit rules universal through d = 12
  (`ladder_rarity.py`), on three digit families: {1,4,6,7}, {1,8,9,9} and {1,2,3,4,8} plus zeros. 146070 is
  the only one of these from the digits of 6174, and {1,4,6,7} is the only family present from d = 4 on
  (the other 4-digit universal digit set, {2,3,5,8}, has no universal fixed point at d = 5).

## General cores (the 551 of AUDIT section 4.5)

For a core (a, b) the extended rule is K_d = |H + L| with the same H and
L = c_0 s_0 + ... + c_4 s_4 - 10^y s_5 + 10^x s_{d-1}, c_i = 10^(a_i) - 10^(b_i), x = a_5, y = b_5, |L| < 10^6
(L is a difference of two six-digit numbers). Lemmas 0, 1 and 3 carry over word for word (Lemma 0 is a
per-core finite check). Lemma 0' carries over: 0 is excluded by the per-core Lemma 0, 9^d by the same
rearrangement argument, and r^d (r = 1..8) by Lemma 1 once d >= 16. The drain needs a restatement, because for small x it is slower and can pass through
zero-free states:

**Lemma 2' (general nine-count rule).** For a state 9^t + R + (zeros) with t >= 6 and smallest digit m <= 8,
L = -10^x (9 - m) < 0 (using sum of the six c_i = 0), the low part is 10^6 - 10^x (9 - m), and the image has
exactly t - 1 - x nines, plus one more if the state is zero-free with m = 8. Its other nonzero digits are at
most 15 (Lemma 1).

**Assembly for general cores.** The 9-count is non-increasing along drain steps and strictly decreases
except in the single stall case (x = 0, zero-free, m = 8), whose image 9^t + zeros has a zero and therefore
strictly decreases at the next step. So the 9-count reaches <= 5 on some image; that image has at most 15
other nonzero digits, hence lies in the seed set S and, having at most 20 nonzero digits, has a zero once
d >= 21. If the core's closure visits at most M nonzero digits, every later state has a zero for d >= M + 1,
Lemma 3 applies throughout, and the finite check finishes the proof for d >= max(21, M + 1).
For x = 0 the drain takes about d steps rather than d / (1 + x); 74 of the 551 cores have x = 0.

M = 20 for 537 cores and 21 for 14. Lengths 6..20 were enumerated for all 551 (`all_cores_summary.txt`);
length 21 for the 14 cores with M = 21 was enumerated twice, in-session and by the referee
(`referee/enumg21_out.txt`): all 14 universal. The 551-core claims in this section (the census, the M values
and the enumerations) were not re-verified in the fourth review.

## Fourth review (2026-11-25)

Verdict: the proof holds; the gaps found were in wording and are fixed above. Recomputed with independent code
written from scratch (`referee_146070_2026-11-25/`): complete enumeration of every non-repdigit multiset at every
length from 6 to 30 (211,915,122 multisets at d = 30), all reaching 146070 with no cycle and no other fixed
point, at most 41 steps to the target digits and 42 to the integer for d >= 8; the closure, with 2,941,883 seeds,
2,942,591 states, maximum 20 nonzero digits and maximum 41 steps; and structured and random inputs at d = 21 to
400, with no failure of the main claim, the step bound, or Lemmas 1 to 3. Not reproduced: the d = 1000
cross-check and the 551-core census. This is an AI review, not a human one.
