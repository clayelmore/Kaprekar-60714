---
title: "Kaprekar-Type Constants for Every Length $L \geq 6$ and Every Base $b \geq 3$, with a Finite Certificate for a Family of Rules"
author: "Clay Elmore"
date: "2026"
abstract: |
  Kaprekar's routine sorts the digits of a number, subtracts the ascending arrangement from the descending one, and repeats; at four digits in base ten every input with at least two distinct digits reaches $6174$. At most other lengths and in most other bases the routine ends in cycles. We study the wider family in which two fixed arrangements of the sorted digits are subtracted. For every length $L \geq 6$ we give an explicit rule of this kind and prove that in every base $b \geq 3$ it sends every string that is not a repdigit to the single value $(b-1)(b^h - 1)$, $h = \lceil L/2 \rceil$; in base ten this is $8991$ at length $6$, $89991$ at lengths $7$ and $8$, $899991$ at lengths $9$ and $10$, and so on. At even length the two arrangements differ by exchanging the two halves of the places, extending a four-digit rule studied by Young, which in bases $b \geq 3$ has a single nonzero constant only when $b = 3 \cdot 2^k$; at odd length, where the places cannot all be exchanged in pairs, one letter is held fixed. For the whole family of such half-swap rules in an even base we prove a finite certificate: a rule on $2n$ digits is universal (every non-repdigit reaches one nonzero fixed value) if and only if its pairs cover every gap and a map on $\binom{n+H}{n}$ states, $H = \lfloor (b-1)/2 \rfloor$, has an attracting fixed state ($126$ states at ten digits in base ten). A subclass of these rules lifts to every longer even length with explicit endpoints. We report a census of the family in base ten, and we prove that two rules which pass in every small base tested fail in infinitely many large ones. The inverse problem, to produce a rule and a proof for a prescribed endpoint, is left open. The theorems are tested by complete enumeration in small cases and the proofs have been reviewed by AI models; they have not yet been reviewed by a mathematician.
